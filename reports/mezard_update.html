Score-Based Diffusion  ·  Progress Report  ·  March 2026

Score Fields for Dynamic Objects

On the propagator structure of the score across internal time — solvable toy models, closed-form results, and the order parameter formulation.

Giovanni Mantovani & Marco Lomele · Università Bocconi Report for Prof. Marc Mézard · March 2026

§1 Problem Setting

Score-based diffusion models are well understood for static data. We study the dynamic case: a dynamic object — a video, a trajectory, a rotating shape — is a sequence of frames \(a^\mu = \{a_u\}_{u=0}^{T-1}\) with \(a_u \in \mathbb{R}^d\). The model must learn the score function jointly across all frames, while consecutive frames are highly correlated. The question is how to exploit this structure.

Two independent notions of time govern the problem. We call this the two-clock architecture.

Internal time   \(u\)

The semantic clock: the frame index, the position along the trajectory. The system evolves according to its own Markovian dynamics (rotation, drift, diffusion). Think of \(u = 0, 1, 2, \ldots, T{-}1\).

Diffusion time   \(t\)

The noise clock: an independent Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process corrupts each frame toward \(\mathcal{N}(0, I_d)\) as \(t \to \infty\), regardless of \(u\).

The forward process corrupts each frame independently:

\[ X_t(u) \;=\; a_u\,e^{-t} \;+\; \sqrt{1 - e^{-2t}}\;Z(u), \qquad Z(u) \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I_d). \]

The per-frame score is then \(s(x, u, t) := \nabla_x \log p_t(x \mid u)\), defined on the full \((x, u, t)\) space.

Propagator hypothesis. Because consecutive frames are highly correlated, we hypothesise the existence of an operator \(P\) — possibly linear, possibly stationary — such that \[ s(\,\cdot\,, u, t) \;\approx\; P\bigl[\,s(\,\cdot\,, u-1, t)\bigr]. \] Identifying \(P\) would reduce the learning problem from \(\mathcal{O}(dT)\) to \(\mathcal{O}(d)\), and would provide a principled parametrisation of sequential diffusion models. We study this in exactly solvable Gaussian settings where all quantities are closed-form.

§3 Main Results

3.1   The rotation propagator   (TM3)

The internal dynamics are \(a(u+1) = R_{\Delta\theta}\,a(u) + \xi_u\), with \(\xi_u \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_\eta^2 I_2)\) and \(R_{\Delta\theta}\) a rotation by angle \(\Delta\theta\). Starting from \(a(0) \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_0, \Sigma_0)\), the marginal at step \(u\) is Gaussian with mean \(\mu_u = R_{\Delta\theta}^u\,\mu_0\) and covariance \(\Sigma_u\) converging to isotropic \(\sigma_\infty^2 I\). After OU diffusion at time \(t\), the per-frame score is:

\[ s(x,\, u,\, t) \;=\; -\,\Sigma_u(t)^{-1}\bigl(x - \mu_u(t)\bigr), \]

where \(\mu_u(t) = e^{-t}\mu_u\) and \(\Sigma_u(t) = e^{-2t}\Sigma_u + (1 - e^{-2t})I\).

In the isotropic limit \(\Sigma_u(t) = \sigma^2(t)\,I\) — reached when \(u\) is large or \(t\) is intermediate — a direct computation gives:

\[ s(x,\, u{+}1,\, t) \;=\; -\frac{x - R_{\Delta\theta}\,\mu_u(t)}{\sigma^2(t)} \;=\; R_{\Delta\theta} \cdot s\!\bigl(R_{-\Delta\theta}\,x,\; u,\; t\bigr). \]
Result. The propagator \(P = R_{\Delta\theta}\) acts as \(\bigl[P \cdot s_u\bigr](x) = R_{\Delta\theta}\cdot s_u(R_{-\Delta\theta}\,x)\): it rotates the evaluation point backwards, then rotates the output forwards. This is exact when the covariance is isotropic, and degrades gracefully otherwise, with an error controlled by the spectral gap of \(\Sigma_u(t)\).

The Jacobian of the score, \(J_s = -\Sigma_u(t)^{-1}\), provides a spectral diagnostic. Its eigenvalues all collapse to \(-1\) as \(t \to \infty\) — the score loses all memory of the dynamics. The identity \(\mathcal{I}(u,t) \,=\, -\lambda_k(J_s) \,=\, 1/\sigma_u^2(t)\) (Fisher information equals minus the Jacobian eigenvalue) holds exactly for all Gaussian models.

3.2   Four regimes on the \((u,t)\) plane   (TM5)

For the univariate AR(1) chain, the closed-form score is \(s(x,u,t) = -(x - \mu_u(t))/\sigma_u^2(t)\), where \(\sigma_u^2(t) = 1 + e^{-2t}(\sigma_u^2 - 1)\). The entire landscape — score norm, Fisher information, entropy — organises into four qualitatively distinct regions of the \((u,t)\) plane.

Phase I   Sharp Phase II   Best window Phase III   Stationary Phase IV   Universal noise
Phase Region What happens to the score
I   Sharp \(u\) small, \(t\) small Score tracks the initial condition closely; strong anisotropy and high Fisher information
II   Best window \(t\) intermediate Variance balances signal and noise; propagator structure is most legible; optimal regime for fitting \(P\)
III   Stationary \(u\) large, \(t\) small Variance converges to \(\sigma_\infty^2\); variance part of propagator \(\to 1\); only the mean still evolves
IV   Universal noise \(t\) large \(\sigma_u^2(t) \to 1\) for all \(u\); score \(\approx -x\); all dynamical information erased

The one-step propagator decomposes cleanly: the mean component \(\mu_{u+1}(t) = \alpha\,\mu_u(t)\) propagates as a pure \(\alpha\)-contraction, entirely independent of \(t\); the variance component \(A(u{+}1,t)/A(u,t) = \sigma_u^2(t)/\sigma_{u+1}^2(t)\) is determined by the AR(1) recursion and equals \(1\) at stationarity.

3.3   Order parameter SDE and the Riccati equation   (TM6)

The circle model is our first non-Gaussian test bed. Because the radial and angular processes are independent, the joint density factorises, \(p_0(r,\theta \mid u) = p_r(r \mid u)\cdot p_\theta(\theta \mid u)\), and the Cartesian score separates into a radial and a tangential part:

\[ s(x, u) \;=\; \Bigl(s_r(r,u) \;-\; \tfrac{1}{r}\Bigr)\hat{e}_r \;+\; \frac{s_\theta(\theta,u)}{r}\,\hat{e}_\theta, \]

where \(s_r = -(r - m_r)/\sigma_r^2\), \(s_\theta = -(\theta - \theta_{\mathrm{mean}})/\sigma_\theta^2\), and the \(-1/r\) correction comes from the Jacobian of the polar change of variables. The score is fully determined by the order parameter \(\varphi(u) = (m_r,\,\sigma_r^2,\,\theta_{\mathrm{mean}},\,\sigma_\theta^2) \in \mathbb{R}^4\), each component satisfying a simple closed ODE.

Introducing the precision \(\kappa = 1/\sigma^2\) (equal to minus the Jacobian eigenvalue), the variance equations translate into a Riccati equation. For the AR(1) model with contraction rate \(\gamma = -\log\alpha\):

\[ \frac{\mathrm{d}\kappa}{\mathrm{d}u} \;=\; 2\gamma\kappa \;-\; \sigma_\eta^2\,\kappa^2. \]

Stable fixed point: \(\kappa_* = 2\gamma/\sigma_\eta^2 = 1/\sigma_\infty^2\). For the circle model (\(\gamma = 0\)): \(\mathrm{d}\kappa_\theta/\mathrm{d}u = -2T_\theta\,\kappa_\theta^2\), giving \(\kappa_\theta(u) = 1/(2T_\theta u)\) — an algebraic decay.

Finally, the relative error of the rotation propagator \(P = R_\omega\) on the tangential score component is computable in closed form:

\[ \varepsilon(u,\,t) \;=\; \frac{2T_\theta}{2T_\theta\,u \;+\; (1 - e^{-2t})}. \]

At \(t = 0\): \(\varepsilon = 1/u\) (algebraic decay in internal time). At intermediate \(t\) (Phase II): the denominator grows, reducing the error further.

Unification. The Riccati equation \(\mathrm{d}\kappa/\mathrm{d}u = 2\gamma\kappa - \sigma_\eta^2\kappa^2\) governs the precision — equivalently, the Jacobian eigenvalue — for all Gaussian internal dynamics. It unifies the AR(1) (\(\gamma > 0\)), the circle (\(\gamma = 0\)), and extends to the matrix case for the rotating AR(1) in \(\mathbb{R}^2\). The score field is entirely parametrised by a finite-dimensional order parameter satisfying a closed dynamical system.

§4 Next Steps

  1. Matrix Riccati for rotating covariance. Derive the full \(2\times 2\) Riccati equation for the precision tensor \(K_u = \Sigma_u^{-1}\) in the rotating AR(1) model. Verify that the rotational symmetry of the steady state forces \(K_\infty \propto I\), recovering the isotropic limit analytically.
  2. Beyond Gaussians. For a two-component Gaussian mixture with rotating means, the score acquires a nonlinear correction to the propagator. Quantify the leading-order departure from \(P = R_{\Delta\theta}\) as a function of the mixture separation and diffusion time \(t\).
  3. Large-\(d\) analysis. Investigate whether the four-regime phase diagram sharpens into genuine phase transitions as \(d \to \infty\), in analogy with the speciation and collapse transitions studied in Biroli–Bonnaire–de Bortoli–Mézard for static data.
  4. Empirical score extraction. Train a small score network on the circle model and compare the learned \(P\) against the analytical rotation propagator. Measure whether Phase II (intermediate \(t\)) is indeed the regime where the learned propagator is most accurate.
