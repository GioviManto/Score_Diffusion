Score Fields for Dynamic Objects

Propagator Structure, Two-Clock Geometry, and Solvable Toy Models

Giovanni Manto & Marco  ·  March 6, 2026  ·  Update for Prof. Marc Mézard

Executive summary. We study how the score field s(x, u, t) = ∇x log pt(x | u) evolves across internal time u (frame index of a dynamic object) for a fixed diffusion time t. Using fully solvable Gaussian toy models — AR(1) chains and rotating AR(1) processes — we derive the exact propagator P mapping s(·, u−1, t) → s(·, u, t), map the four dynamical regimes on the (u, t) plane, and identify the intermediate-t window where P is clearest. The next step is to write a closed SDE for the order parameter (mean and covariance) evolving in u, with the score as a driven functional.

§1 Goal & Framing

Setting. A dynamic object (video, trajectory) is a sequence of frames aμ = {au}T−1u=0, au ∈ ℝd. Score-based generative models for such objects must learn s(x, u, t) := ∇x log pt(x | u) jointly for all frames. The naive approach embeds the full trajectory in ℝdT; we aim instead to exploit the high cross-frame correlation.

Two-clock architecture. Two independent time parameters govern the problem:

u ∈ {0, 1, …, T−1} internal time (semantic / physical evolution of the object)
t ≥ 0 diffusion time (OU noise clock, independent per frame)

Forward: X_t(u) = a_u e^{−t} + √(1−e^{−2t}) Z(u), Z(u) ~ N(0, I_d)

Propagator hypothesis. Given strong temporal correlations, we hypothesize a (possibly linear, stationary) operator P such that:

S_{:,u}(x) ≈ P( S_{:,u−1}(x) ), where S_{:,u}(x) := s(x, u, t) at fixed t

P could be a rotation, a contraction, or more generally a functional of the underlying SDE generator. Learning P would reduce the cost from O(dT) to O(d) per generation step.

§3 Toy Model 3 — Rotation as an Exact Propagator

a) Setup

The internal-time dynamics are a discrete rotating AR(1) process in ℝ²:

a(u+1) = RΔθ · a(u) + ξu, ξu ~ N(0, σ²η I₂)

RΔθ = [ cos Δθ −sin Δθ ]    (rotation by Δθ per step)       [ sin Δθ   cos Δθ ]

Starting from a(0) ~ N(μ₀, Σ₀), the marginal at step u is Gaussian N(μu, Σu) with:

μu = (RΔθ)^u · μ₀    (rotates at speed Δθ per step)
Σu = (RΔθ)^u Σ₀ (RΔθ)^{u,T} + σ²∞ I   (converges to isotropic σ²∞ = σ²η/(1−1) limit)

Each frame is independently corrupted by OU noise: Xt(u) = a(u)e−t + √(1−e−2t) Z, giving marginal N(μu(t), Σu(t)) where μu(t) = e−tμu and Σu(t) = e−2tΣu + (1−e−2t)I.

b) Exact score and propagator

The per-frame score is:

s(x, u, t) = − Σu(t)⁻¹ · (x − μu(t)) ✓ exact

Key observation. In the isotropic limit (Σu(t) = σ²(t) I, i.e. the stationary or high-t regime), the score becomes purely x-linear with scalar coefficient:

s(x, u+1, t) = −[x − RΔθ μu(t)] / σ²(t)
= RΔθ · [−(R−Δθx − μu(t)) / σ²(t)]
= RΔθ · s(R−Δθ x, u, t) ✓ exact for isotropic Σ

The propagator P = RΔθ acts simultaneously on the output of the score and on its argument x: [P·su](x) = RΔθ · s(R−Δθx, u, t). This is exact at stationarity (large u and/or intermediate t) and approximate otherwise.

c) Spectral / Jacobian evidence

The Jacobian of the score w.r.t. x is Js = −Σu(t)⁻¹, with eigenvalues λk(u,t) = −1/λk(Σu(t)). As t → ∞: λk(Σu(t)) → 1 for all k, so all Jacobian eigenvalues collapse to −1 (dimensional collapse: score becomes isotropic, indistinguishable from a standard Gaussian). The spectral gap — the difference between the two eigenvalues — is a diagnostic of how anisotropic (data-rich) the score still is. It is visualized in Lab 3 of TM5.

d) Interpretation in diffusion-time regimes

The rotation propagator P = RΔθ is: (i) exact in Phase II (intermediate t, near stationarity in u); (ii) approximate at small t (where Σ is still anisotropic, so the isotropic assumption fails); (iii) trivial at large t (dimensional collapse, s ≈ −x, no memory of rotation angle). This confirms Achilli's intuition: intermediate t is the best window to study P.

§4 Toy Model 5 — The Two-Clock Phase Diagram

a) Setup: AR(1) + OU

Univariate AR(1) process: xu+1 = αxu + ηu, α ∈ (0,1), ηu ~ N(0, σ²η). Closed-form marginals:

μu = α^u μ₀,   σ²u = σ²∞ + α^{2u}(σ²0 − σ²∞),   σ²∞ = σ²η/(1−α²)

After OU noise: μu(t) = e^{−t} μu,   σ²u(t) = 1 + e^{−2t}(σ²u−1)

b) Exact score, Jacobian, and information triad

s(x, u, t) = −(x − μu(t)) / σ²u(t)

Js(u,t) = ∂s/∂x = −1/σ²u(t) = −I(u,t) [Fisher information]

H(u,t) = ½ log(2πe σ²u(t)) [differential entropy]

Triad: I(u,t) = −Js(u,t) = 1/σ²u(t) (for Gaussians)

c) The four-regime phase diagram ✓ verified in Lab 2

Phase I — Sharp
small u, small t: distribution tracks initial condition closely; high Fisher info; score anisotropic and data-rich.
Phase II — Best window
any u, intermediate t: variance σ²u(t) balances data signal and noise; propagator P most visible; cleanest regime for studying temporal coherence.
Phase III — Stationary
large u, small t: σ²u → σ²∞; score is time-u–independent in variance; only mean still evolves.
Phase IV — Universal noise
large t: σ²u(t) → 1 for all u; s(x,u,t) ≈ −x; all information about u and dynamics lost.

d) Propagator analysis for AR(1)

The score is linear in x: s(x,u,t) = A(u,t)·x + b(u,t) with:

A(u,t) = −1/σ²u(t) (Jacobian, scalar)
b(u,t) = μu(t)/σ²u(t) (mean contribution)

The one-step propagator decomposes into two parts:

  • Variance part: A(u+1,t)/A(u,t) = σ²u(t)/σ²u+1(t) — deterministic, computable from the AR(1) variance recursion.
  • Mean part: μu+1(t) = α · μu(t) — simple contraction by α, independent of t. The score's mean-shift component propagates exactly with factor α.

Insight: The propagator Pt depends on t only through the variance ratio; the mean propagation (α contraction) is t-independent. At stationarity (σ²u ≈ σ²u+1 ≈ σ²∞) the variance factor → 1 and the propagator reduces to a pure α-rescaling of the score's "mean" component. hypothesis This structure should persist for non-Gaussian priors up to corrections from mixture nonlinearity.

§5 What We Now Understand

Score vs internal time u:

  • For Gaussian dynamics, the per-frame score s(x,u,t) remains linear in x for all u, with coefficients (−A, b) obeying closed deterministic ODEs driven by the AR(1) recursion.
  • For the rotation model, the score field rotates rigidly: [P·su](x) = RΔθ·s(R−Δθx, u, t). This is exact in the isotropic-covariance regime.
  • The mean propagation is always exact and t-independent: μu+1(t) = α·μu(t); correspondingly, the score's "offset" component propagates as a pure α-contraction.

Dependence on diffusion time t (early / late):

  • At small t: score is strongly anisotropic and data-specific; the propagator P carries full information about the dynamics (rotation, contraction, covariance).
  • At intermediate t (Phase II): the propagator is cleanest — covariance near stationary, mean still signal-bearing — optimal regime for learning or fitting P from data.
  • At large t (Phase IV): dimensional collapse; s ≈ −x for all u; P ≈ identity, all dynamical structure erased. Corresponds to the "speciation collapse" in Mézard–Biroli language.

Spectral / Jacobian insights:

  • The triad identity I(u,t) = −Js(u,t) = 1/σ²u(t) holds exactly for all Gaussian toy models.
  • Spectral gap λ1(J) − λ2(J) = 1/σ²2(u,t) − 1/σ²1(u,t) is a direct diagnostic of "how much anisotropy / dynamical information remains in the score".
  • Dimensional collapse (all λk → −1) is sharp and monotone in t, suggesting a clean analog of the speciation/collapse transition in our toy setting.
