Score-Based Diffusion Models for Dynamic Objects

Propagator structure across internal time — progress report

Giovanni Manto & Marco Benedetti

École Normale Supérieure – PSL

March 2026  ·  Report for Prof. Marc Mézard

Abstract. We study how the per-frame score field $s(x, u, t) \;{:=}\; \nabla_x \log p_t(x \mid u)$ of a score-based diffusion model evolves across internal time $u$ (the frame index of a dynamic object such as a video or trajectory), at fixed diffusion time $t$. Working in fully solvable Gaussian settings — AR(1) chains and rotating AR(1) processes — we derive the exact propagator $P$ that maps $s(\cdot, u-1, t)$ to $s(\cdot, u, t)$, characterise the four dynamical regimes of the joint $(u,t)$ plane, and identify the intermediate-$t$ window where $P$ takes its simplest form. We further introduce the circle model as the first non-Gaussian test bed, where the score separates into radial and angular components, and we derive a closed Riccati equation governing the evolution of the score's precision tensor across $u$.

1. Setting and Motivation

Score-based generative models have been developed primarily for static objects such as images. We are interested in the case of dynamic objects — short sequences of frames forming a trajectory — where a key structure is available: consecutive frames are highly correlated. A trajectory of length $T$ is a sequence $a^\mu = \{a_u\}_{u=0}^{T-1}$ with $a_u \in \R^d$. The naive approach embeds the full object in $\R^{dT}$; we instead aim to exploit the temporal correlations explicitly.

Two independent time parameters govern the problem. Internal time $u \in \{0,\ldots,T{-}1\}$ describes the physical or semantic evolution of the object. Diffusion time $t \geq 0$ is the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck (OU) noise clock applied independently to each frame: $$ X_t(u) \;=\; a_u\,e^{-t} \;+\; \sqrt{1 - e^{-2t}}\;Z(u), \qquad Z(u) \sim \N(0, I_d). $$ The per-frame score is $s(x, u, t) := \nabla_x \log p_t(x \mid u)$.

The central question is whether there exists a propagator $P$ — possibly linear, stationary, and related to the generator of the underlying dynamics — such that $$ s(\cdot,\, u,\, t) \;\approx\; P\bigl[s(\cdot,\, u-1,\, t)\bigr]. $$ Identifying $P$ would reduce score learning from an $O(dT)$ problem to an $O(d)$ problem, and would provide a principled parametrisation of video diffusion models.

3. Rotating AR(1): an Exact Propagator

Model and exact score.

The internal dynamics are a discrete rotating AR(1) process in $\R^2$: $$ a(u+1) = R_{\Delta\theta}\,a(u) + \xi_u, \qquad \xi_u \sim \N(0,\,\sigma_\eta^2 I_2), $$ where $R_{\Delta\theta}$ is the rotation matrix by angle $\Delta\theta$. Starting from $a(0) \sim \N(\mu_0, \Sigma_0)$, the marginal at step $u$ is Gaussian $\N(\mu_u, \Sigma_u)$ with $\mu_u = R_{\Delta\theta}^u\,\mu_0$ and $\Sigma_u = R_{\Delta\theta}^u\,\Sigma_0\,R_{\Delta\theta}^{-u} + \sigma_\infty^2 I$ (converging to isotropic variance $\sigma_\infty^2 = \sigma_\eta^2$). After OU diffusion at time $t$, the marginal is $\N(\mu_u(t), \Sigma_u(t))$ with $\mu_u(t) = e^{-t}\mu_u$ and $\Sigma_u(t) = e^{-2t}\Sigma_u + (1-e^{-2t})I$. The per-frame score is $$ s(x,u,t) \;=\; -\,\Sigma_u(t)^{-1}\bigl(x - \mu_u(t)\bigr). $$

The rotation propagator.

In the isotropic limit $\Sigma_u(t) = \sigma^2(t)\,I$ — reached for large $u$ or intermediate $t$ — one finds $$ s(x,\,u{+}1,\,t) \;=\; -\frac{x - R_{\Delta\theta}\,\mu_u(t)}{\sigma^2(t)} \;=\; R_{\Delta\theta}\cdot s\!\bigl(R_{-\Delta\theta}\,x,\;u,\;t\bigr). $$

Result. The propagator $P = R_{\Delta\theta}$ acts on the score field as $[P\cdot s_u](x) = R_{\Delta\theta}\cdot s_u(R_{-\Delta\theta}\,x)$: it rotates both the evaluation point and the output vector. This is exact when the covariance is isotropic, and approximates the true $s_{u+1}$ with an error controlled by the anisotropy of $\Sigma_u(t)$.

Jacobian and dimensional collapse.

The Jacobian of the score with respect to $x$ is $J_s(u,t) = -\Sigma_u(t)^{-1}$, with eigenvalues $\lambda_k^J = -1/\lambda_k(\Sigma_u(t))$. As $t\to\infty$ all eigenvalues of $\Sigma_u(t)$ tend to $1$, so all Jacobian eigenvalues collapse to $-1$: the score becomes isotropic and loses all memory of the dynamics. The spectral gap $\lambda_1^J - \lambda_2^J$ is a natural diagnostic of how much dynamical information the score still carries. The triad identity $\mathcal{I}(u,t) = -\lambda_k^J = 1/\sigma^2_u(t)$ (Fisher information equals minus the Jacobian eigenvalue) holds exactly for Gaussian models.

4. The Two-Clock Phase Diagram

AR(1) with OU diffusion.

For the univariate AR(1) chain $x_{u+1} = \alpha x_u + \eta_u$ ($\alpha \in (0,1)$, $\eta_u \sim \N(0,\sigma_\eta^2)$), the marginal is Gaussian for all $u$ with $\mu_u = \alpha^u \mu_0$ and $\sigma_u^2 = \sigma_\infty^2 + \alpha^{2u}(\sigma_0^2 - \sigma_\infty^2)$, where $\sigma_\infty^2 = \sigma_\eta^2/(1-\alpha^2)$. After OU noise, $\mu_u(t) = e^{-t}\mu_u$ and $\sigma_u^2(t) = 1 + e^{-2t}(\sigma_u^2 - 1)$. The exact score is $s(x,u,t) = -(x-\mu_u(t))/\sigma_u^2(t)$.

The score is linear in $x$: $s = A(u,t)\,x + b(u,t)$ with Jacobian $A = -1/\sigma_u^2(t)$ and offset $b = \mu_u(t)/\sigma_u^2(t)$. The propagator decomposes as: (i) the mean part $\mu_{u+1}(t) = \alpha\,\mu_u(t)$, an exact $\alpha$-contraction that is entirely independent of $t$; (ii) the variance part $A(u{+}1,t)/A(u,t) = \sigma_u^2(t)/\sigma_{u+1}^2(t)$, determined by the AR(1) variance recursion. At stationarity ($\sigma_u^2 \approx \sigma_{u+1}^2 \approx \sigma_\infty^2$), the variance ratio tends to $1$ and the propagator reduces to a pure rescaling by $\alpha$.

Four regimes on the $(u,t)$ plane.

The scalar function $\sigma_u^2(t)$ — and hence the entire score — organises into four qualitatively distinct regions, confirmed by interactive inspection of the heatmaps in TM5.

Phase Region Character
I  Sharp $u$ small, $t$ small Score tracks initial condition closely; high Fisher information; strong anisotropy
II  Best window $t$ intermediate Variance balances signal and noise; propagator structure most legible; optimal regime for fitting $P$
III  Stationary $u$ large, $t$ small $\sigma_u^2 \to \sigma_\infty^2$; variance part of propagator $\to 1$; only mean still evolves
IV  Universal noise $t$ large $\sigma_u^2(t) \to 1$ for all $u$; $s(x,u,t) \approx -x$; all dynamical structure erased

5. Circle Model: Order Parameter SDE

The circle model is our first test bed with non-Gaussian geometry. The two-dimensional process $(r(u), \theta(u))$ evolves according to independent SDEs: $$ \d r = (1-r)\,\d u + \sqrt{2T_r}\,\d B_r, \qquad \d\theta = \omega\,\d u + \sqrt{2T_\theta}\,\d B_\theta, $$ with $T_r$ setting the radial confinement strength and $T_\theta$ the angular diffusion.

Separable exact score.

Because $r$ and $\theta$ are independent, the joint density factorises: $p_0(r,\theta \mid u) = p_r(r \mid u)\cdot p_\theta(\theta \mid u)$. In Cartesian coordinates $(x,y) = (r\cos\theta, r\sin\theta)$, the score separates into radial and tangential components: $$ s(x,u) \;=\; \Bigl(s_r(r,u) - \tfrac{1}{r}\Bigr)\hat{e}_r \;+\; \frac{s_\theta^{\scriptscriptstyle 1D}(\theta,u)}{r}\,\hat{e}_\theta, $$ where $s_r = -(r - m_r)/\sigma_r^2$, $s_\theta^{\scriptscriptstyle 1D} = -(\theta - \theta_{\mathrm{mean}})/\sigma_\theta^2$, and the $-1/r$ correction arises from the Jacobian of the polar change of variables. The order parameters $\varphi(u) = (m_r, \sigma_r^2, \theta_{\mathrm{mean}}, \sigma_\theta^2)$ satisfy a closed, linear ODE system that can be read off from the two SDEs above.

Riccati equation for precision.

Let $\kappa_\theta(u) = 1/\sigma_\theta^2(u)$ denote the angular precision (equal to minus the tangential Jacobian eigenvalue). Since $\sigma_\theta^2(u) = 2T_\theta u$, the precision satisfies $$ \frac{\d\kappa_\theta}{\d u} \;=\; -2T_\theta\,\kappa_\theta^2, \qquad \kappa_\theta(u) = \frac{1}{2T_\theta u}. $$ For the contracting AR(1) model (contraction rate $\gamma = -\log\alpha > 0$), the radial precision $\kappa = 1/\sigma^2$ satisfies the Riccati equation $$ \frac{\d\kappa}{\d u} \;=\; 2\gamma\kappa \;-\; \sigma_\eta^2\,\kappa^2, $$ whose stable fixed point is $\kappa_* = 2\gamma/\sigma_\eta^2 = 1/\sigma_\infty^2$.

Result. The Riccati equation $\d\kappa/\d u = 2\gamma\kappa - \sigma_\eta^2\kappa^2$ governs the evolution of the score's precision (equivalently, its Jacobian eigenvalue) for all Gaussian internal dynamics. It unifies the AR(1) ($\gamma > 0$) and circle ($\gamma = 0$) models. The score field is entirely determined by the order parameter vector $\varphi(u) \in \R^4$ — a closed, finite-dimensional description.

Analytical propagator error.

The rotation propagator $P = R_\omega$ fails whenever the angular variance changes between steps, i.e. when $T_\theta > 0$. A direct calculation gives the exact relative error on the tangential score component: $$ \varepsilon(u, t) \;=\; \frac{2T_\theta}{2T_\theta\,u \;+\; (1 - e^{-2t})}. $$ At $t = 0$ this simplifies to $\varepsilon(u, 0) = 1/u$, an algebraic decay. The denominator grows with diffusion time $t$ because OU noise broadens the effective angular distribution, making the ratio $\sigma_\theta^2(u{+}1)/\sigma_\theta^2(u)$ closer to $1$. This confirms quantitatively that Phase II (intermediate $t$, large $u$) is the regime where $P = R_\omega$ is most accurate.

6. Summary of Results

The following points represent what we currently understand with certainty from the analytical toy models.

  • Linearity in $x$. For all Gaussian internal dynamics, $s(x,u,t)$ is linear in $x$ for every $u$ and $t$, with coefficients obeying closed ODEs. The propagator $P$ maps linear fields to linear fields and is parametrised by two scalars (a variance ratio and the contraction $\alpha$).
  • Rotation propagator. For rotating AR(1), the propagator $[P\cdot s_u](x) = R_{\Delta\theta}\cdot s_u(R_{-\Delta\theta}x)$ is exact in the isotropic-covariance limit and has a quantifiable error otherwise.
  • $t$-independence of mean propagation. The shift $\mu_{u+1}(t) = \alpha\,\mu_u(t)$ holds for all $t$: the mean component of the propagator does not depend on the diffusion time.
  • Triad identity. Fisher information $= -$Jacobian eigenvalue $= 1/\sigma_u^2(t)$, exactly, for all Gaussian toy models. This identity provides three equivalent diagnostics for the same quantity.
  • Dimensional collapse. All Jacobian eigenvalues $\to -1$ as $t \to \infty$. The spectral gap is a monotone measure of how much dynamical structure remains visible in the score at noise level $t$.
  • Riccati unification. The evolution of the score's precision in internal time $u$ is governed by a Riccati equation, closing the description at the level of order parameters.

7. Open Questions and Next Steps

  1. Matrix Riccati and rotating covariance. Derive the full $2\times 2$ Riccati equation for the precision tensor $K_u = \Sigma_u^{-1}$ in the rotating AR(1) model, and verify that the rotation invariance of the steady state implies $K_\infty \propto I$.
  2. Beyond Gaussians. For a two-component Gaussian mixture with rotating means, the score acquires a nonlinear correction. Characterise when the rotation propagator remains a good approximation and quantify the leading-order departure.
  3. Large-$d$ limit. Investigate whether the four-regime phase diagram sharpens into genuine phase transitions as $d \to \infty$, in analogy with the speciation and collapse transitions of Biroli–Bonnaire–de Bortoli–Mézard for static data.
  4. Stochastic generalisation. Extend the Riccati analysis to the case where the internal dynamics are themselves random (i.e.\ where $\alpha$ or $\omega$ fluctuate), and study how the propagator $P$ becomes distribution-valued.
