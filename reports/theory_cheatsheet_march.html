0. The Central Problem (30-second version)

A dynamic object (video, trajectory) is a sequence of frames aμ={au}u=0T−1a^\mu = \{a_u\}_{u=0}^{T-1}aμ={au​}u=0T−1​. You want a generative model that can denoise any frame. Score-based diffusion does this by learning s(x,u,t)=∇xlog⁡pt(x∣u)s(x, u, t) = \nabla_x \log p_t(x \mid u)s(x,u,t)=∇x​logpt​(x∣u). The key question: does the score field propagate predictably from one frame to the next, and if so, what is the operator PPP that does it?

1. Score-Based Diffusion (Static Baseline)

Definition. For a distribution p0p_0p0​, the forward (noising) OU process produces ptp_tpt​ at time ttt. The score is:
s(x,t)=∇xlog⁡pt(x)s(x, t) = \nabla_x \log p_t(x)s(x,t)=∇x​logpt​(x)
Denoising = running the reverse SDE driven by sss.

Why it matters. The score tells you: "which direction makes this sample more probable?" Training a score network is equivalent to training a denoiser (Tweedie identity). This is the foundation everything else builds on.

Intuition. Think of the score as a vector field on Rd\mathbb{R}^dRd pointing toward high-density regions. At large ttt, all information is erased and s≈−xs \approx -xs≈−x. At small ttt, the field has sharp structure reflecting the data geometry.

4. Two-Clock Architecture

Definition. Two independent indices govern the problem:

Clock Symbol Role
Internal time u∈{0,…,T−1}u \in \{0, \ldots, T-1\}u∈{0,…,T−1} Semantic: frame index, trajectory position
Diffusion time t∈[0,∞)t \in [0, \infty)t∈[0,∞) Noise level: how corrupted is the frame

Implication. The score s(x,u,t)s(x, u, t)s(x,u,t) lives on a two-dimensional time plane. Its behavior in uuu and ttt is structurally different: ttt controls the noise level of any single frame; uuu controls the correlations between frames through the internal dynamics.

Key insight. These clocks commute: the OU noising is applied after the internal dynamics, and independently per frame. This means pt(x∣u)p_t(x \mid u)pt​(x∣u) is just the noised version of p0(au)p_0(a_u)p0​(au​) — the internal dynamics enter only through the marginal p0(⋅∣u)p_0(\cdot \mid u)p0​(⋅∣u).

5. Propagator Hypothesis

Definition. We hypothesize an operator PPP (possibly linear, possibly ttt-dependent) such that:
s(⋅,u,t)≈P[s(⋅,u−1,t)]s(\cdot, u, t) \approx P\bigl[s(\cdot, u-1, t)\bigr]s(⋅,u,t)≈P[s(⋅,u−1,t)]

Why this matters. If exact, the score at all TTT frames is determined by s(⋅,0,t)s(\cdot, 0, t)s(⋅,0,t) plus PPP — reducing the learning problem from O(dT)\mathcal{O}(dT)O(dT) parameters to O(d)\mathcal{O}(d)O(d). This is the central hypothesis of the project.

What has been confirmed so far:

  • H1 (Linear P): For Gaussian dynamics, the propagator maps linear fields to linear fields. Exact. ✓
  • H2 (Rotation P): For rotating AR(1) at isotropic covariance, [P⋅su](x)=RΔθ⋅s(R−Δθx,u,t)[P \cdot s_u](x) = R_{\Delta\theta} \cdot s(R_{-\Delta\theta} x, u, t)[P⋅su​](x)=RΔθ​⋅s(R−Δθ​x,u,t). Exact at stationarity. ✓
  • H3 (Mean contraction): μu+1(t)=α⋅μu(t)\mu_{u+1}(t) = \alpha \cdot \mu_u(t)μu+1​(t)=α⋅μu​(t), independent of ttt. Exact. ✓

Intuition. PPP is the operator that "moves the score field" the same way the underlying dynamics move the data. For rotation: rotate the input point backward, apply the score, rotate the output forward.

15. Speciation and Collapse Transitions (Biroli et al.)

Context. For static score-based models, Biroli–Bonnaire–de Bortoli–Mézard identify two critical diffusion times in the reverse process:

  • Collapse transition tCt_CtC​: above this, the reverse SDE converges to pure noise; below, it starts to localize
  • Speciation transition tS<tCt_S < t_CtS​<tC​: below this, individual modes of p0p_0p0​ separate out; the score starts "choosing" between clusters

Connection to this project. The four-regime diagram in TM5 is a dynamic analog:

  • Phase IV (universal noise) corresponds to t>tCt > t_Ct>tC​: no structural information
  • Phase II (best window) is analogous to the intermediate regime between tSt_StS​ and tCt_CtC​
  • The open question: do the regime boundaries in the (u,t)(u,t)(u,t) plane sharpen into genuine phase transitions as d→∞d \to \inftyd→∞? In static models they do (mean-field/large-ddd limit).

Why it matters for direction. If the Phase II boundary sharpens as d→∞d \to \inftyd→∞, then there is a phase transition in the propagator accuracy — a clean signal for the theory. This is the large-ddd extension (Next Steps item 3).
