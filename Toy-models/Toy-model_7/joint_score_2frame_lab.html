The Joint Score: Two Frames

Why the propagator requires the full joint distribution — exact derivation for K = 2, AR(1) + OU diffusion

01 The Core Error, Stated Precisely

In toy models TM3–TM6 we computed, for each internal time index \(u\), the marginal score:

$$s^\text{marg}(x_u,\, u,\, t) \;=\; \nabla_{x_u} \log p_t(x_u \mid u)$$
OLD formulation — marginal score at frame u

where \(p_t(x_u \mid u)\) is obtained by integrating out all other frames: $$p_t(x_u \mid u) \;=\; \int \mathrm{d}a_u \; p_0(a_u \mid u)\; \mathcal{N}\!\left(x_u;\, a_u e^{-t},\, \Delta_t\right).$$ This is the distribution you would get if you took a single snapshot at index \(u\) and forgot that it belongs to a sequence. Crucially, the score \(s^\text{marg}(x_u, u, t)\) is a function of \(x_u\) alone — it sees no information from the other frames \(x_{u' \neq u}\).

Why this is wrong for the propagator. We are studying video/trajectory generation: the model generates an entire sequence \((x_0, x_1, \ldots, x_{K-1})\) as a single sample from a joint distribution \(P_0\) over sequences. The score that drives the reverse diffusion is the gradient of the log of this joint distribution, not of its marginals. Computing marginal scores is equivalent to assuming the frames are statistically independent — which contradicts the very correlation structure we are trying to study.

The correct object is:

$$\mathbf{S}(x_0,\ldots,x_{K-1},\, t) \;=\; \nabla_{(x_0,\ldots,x_{K-1})} \log P_t(x_0,\ldots,x_{K-1})$$
CORRECT formulation — joint score, a vector in ℝ^{dK}

The \(k\)-th block component (Mézard, board, 2026-03-12) is: $$S_k(x, t) \;=\; \frac{e^{-t}\,\langle a_k \rangle_{a \mid x} \;-\; x_k}{\Delta_t}$$ where \(\langle a_k \rangle_{a \mid x}\) is the expectation of the clean frame \(a_k\) conditioned on the full noisy trajectory \(x = (x_0,\ldots,x_{K-1})\). This is the optimal Bayesian denoiser for frame \(k\) given all observations simultaneously — a Kalman smoother.

02 Setup: AR(1) + OU, Two Frames (K = 2)

We take the simplest non-trivial case: two frames \((a_0, a_1) \in \mathbb{R}^2\). The internal dynamics is a one-step AR(1) process:

$$a_1 \;=\; \alpha\, a_0 \;+\; \eta, \qquad \eta \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\, \sigma_\eta^2),$$
AR(1) transition, one step. α ∈ (0,1) is the contraction; σ_η² is the innovation noise.

with initial distribution \(a_0 \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_0, \sigma_0^2)\). The full dataset is a collection of pairs \(\{(a_0^\mu, a_1^\mu)\}_{\mu=1}^n\), all drawn from the same joint law.

Step 1 — Joint distribution of clean frames

Since \(a_1 = \alpha a_0 + \eta\) with \(\eta \perp a_0\), the pair \((a_0, a_1)\) is jointly Gaussian:

$$\begin{pmatrix} a_0 \\ a_1 \end{pmatrix} \sim \mathcal{N}\!\left( \begin{pmatrix} \mu_0 \\ \alpha\mu_0 \end{pmatrix},\; \Sigma_0 \right)$$
Joint clean distribution. Mean of a₁ = α·(mean of a₀) by linearity.

where the joint covariance \(\Sigma_0\) has entries:

$$\Sigma_0 \;=\; \begin{pmatrix} \sigma_0^2 & \alpha\,\sigma_0^2 \\ \alpha\,\sigma_0^2 & \alpha^2\sigma_0^2 + \sigma_\eta^2 \end{pmatrix}$$
Derivation: Var(a₀) = σ₀²; Var(a₁) = Var(αa₀+η) = α²σ₀²+σ_η²; Cov(a₀,a₁) = E[(a₀−μ₀)(αa₀+η−αμ₀)] = αE[(a₀−μ₀)²] + E[(a₀−μ₀)·η] = ασ₀²+0.

The off-diagonal entry \(\alpha\sigma_0^2\) encodes the correlation. If \(\alpha = 0\), frames are independent and \(\Sigma_0\) is diagonal.

Step 2 — Forward (OU) diffusion applied independently to each frame

We corrupt each frame independently with the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process. At diffusion time \(t\): $$x_k \;=\; a_k\, e^{-t} \;+\; \sqrt{\Delta_t}\, z_k, \qquad z_k \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1),\quad z_0 \perp z_1,$$ where \(\Delta_t \equiv 1 - e^{-2t}\). The noise is added independently to each frame — there is no correlation in the added noise. Only the clean data \((a_0, a_1)\) is correlated.

The joint noisy distribution \(P_t(x_0, x_1)\) is therefore:

$$P_t(x_0, x_1) \;=\; \int\!\!\int \mathrm{d}a_0\,\mathrm{d}a_1\; P_0(a_0,a_1)\; \mathcal{N}(x_0;\, a_0 e^{-t},\, \Delta_t)\; \mathcal{N}(x_1;\, a_1 e^{-t},\, \Delta_t)$$
Marginalise over all possible clean pairs (a₀,a₁). Since everything is Gaussian, this integral is itself Gaussian.

Since both the prior \(P_0\) and the noise are Gaussian, \(P_t\) is again Gaussian. We compute its parameters by the standard rules for linear transformations of Gaussians. Writing \(x = e^{-t} a + \sqrt{\Delta_t} z\):

$$P_t(x_0, x_1) \;=\; \mathcal{N}\!\left( \begin{pmatrix} x_0 \\ x_1 \end{pmatrix};\; e^{-t}\begin{pmatrix}\mu_0 \\ \alpha\mu_0\end{pmatrix},\; \Sigma_t \right)$$
Joint noisy distribution at diffusion time t.

The key result is the joint covariance at diffusion time \(t\):

$$\boxed{\Sigma_t \;=\; e^{-2t}\,\Sigma_0 \;+\; \Delta_t\, I_2}$$ $$= \begin{pmatrix} \sigma_0^2 e^{-2t} + \Delta_t & \alpha\,\sigma_0^2 e^{-2t} \\ \alpha\,\sigma_0^2 e^{-2t} & (\alpha^2\sigma_0^2+\sigma_\eta^2)\,e^{-2t} + \Delta_t \end{pmatrix}$$
Derivation: Cov(x_i, x_j) = e^{-2t}Cov(a_i,a_j) + Δ_t·δ_{ij}. The first term comes from the signal; the second from the independent OU noise. The off-diagonal is e^{-2t}·ασ₀² — it decays with diffusion time.

Define the three scalar entries for compactness: $$A \;\equiv\; \sigma_0^2 e^{-2t} + \Delta_t, \qquad B \;\equiv\; \alpha\sigma_0^2 e^{-2t}, \qquad C \;\equiv\; (\alpha^2\sigma_0^2+\sigma_\eta^2)e^{-2t} + \Delta_t.$$ Then \(\Sigma_t = \begin{pmatrix} A & B \\ B & C \end{pmatrix}\). Note that \(B = \alpha\sigma_0^2 e^{-2t}\) is the entire inter-frame coupling. At \(t\to\infty\), \(B\to 0\). At \(t=0\), \(B = \alpha\sigma_0^2\) (maximum coupling).

Step 3 — The joint score (exact closed form)

For a Gaussian \(\mathcal{N}(x;\, \mu,\, \Sigma)\), the score is simply \(\nabla_x \log p = -\Sigma^{-1}(x - \mu)\). Applying this to our \(P_t\):

$$\mathbf{S}(x_0, x_1,\, t) \;=\; -\,\Sigma_t^{-1}\begin{pmatrix} x_0 - e^{-t}\mu_0 \\ x_1 - \alpha e^{-t}\mu_0 \end{pmatrix}$$
Joint score = negative precision matrix times centred observation.

The inverse of the 2×2 matrix \(\Sigma_t = \begin{pmatrix}A&B\\B&C\end{pmatrix}\) is:

$$\Sigma_t^{-1} \;=\; \frac{1}{D}\begin{pmatrix} C & -B \\ -B & A \end{pmatrix}, \qquad D \;\equiv\; \det\Sigma_t = AC - B^2.$$
Standard 2×2 matrix inverse. D = AC−B² is the determinant.

Writing \(\tilde{x}_0 = x_0 - e^{-t}\mu_0\) and \(\tilde{x}_1 = x_1 - \alpha e^{-t}\mu_0\) for the centred observations, the two components of the joint score are:

$$\boxed{S_0(x_0, x_1, t) \;=\; -\frac{C\,\tilde{x}_0 \;-\; B\,\tilde{x}_1}{D}}$$ $$\boxed{S_1(x_0, x_1, t) \;=\; -\frac{-B\,\tilde{x}_0 \;+\; A\,\tilde{x}_1}{D}}$$
Exact joint score components. Both depend on BOTH x₀ and x₁ through the off-diagonal coupling B.

03 Old vs New: The Coupling Term

The old marginal scores treated each frame as independent. For frame 0, marginalising \(a_1\) gives \(p_t(x_0 \mid 0) = \mathcal{N}(x_0;\, e^{-t}\mu_0,\, A)\), so:

$$s_0^\text{marg}(x_0, t) \;=\; -\frac{\tilde{x}_0}{A}, \qquad s_1^\text{marg}(x_1, t) \;=\; -\frac{\tilde{x}_1}{C}$$
OLD per-frame marginal scores. Each depends on only one argument.

The joint score component \(S_0\) differs from \(s_0^\text{marg}\) by an explicit coupling term. Computing the difference: $$S_0 \;-\; s_0^\text{marg} \;=\; -\frac{C\tilde{x}_0 - B\tilde{x}_1}{D} + \frac{\tilde{x}_0}{A} \;=\; \tilde{x}_0\!\left(\frac{1}{A} - \frac{C}{D}\right) + \frac{B\tilde{x}_1}{D}$$ Using \(D = AC - B^2\), so \(1/A - C/D = (D-AC)/(AD) = -B^2/(AD)\):

$$S_0 \;-\; s_0^\text{marg} \;=\; \frac{B}{D}\!\left(\tilde{x}_1 \;-\; \frac{B}{A}\,\tilde{x}_0\right)$$
The coupling term. B/D is the coupling strength (vanishes as t→∞ since B→0). The factor (x̃₁ − (B/A)x̃₀) is the Kalman "innovation": the residual of x₁ after predicting it from x₀ using the linear predictor B/A.
Physical meaning of the coupling term.
  • \(B/D\) — coupling strength: proportional to \(\alpha\sigma_0^2 e^{-2t}\), which is the inter-frame correlation in the signal. It vanishes as \(t\to\infty\) (noise dominates, frames become uncorrelated in \(x\)) and is largest at \(t=0\).
  • \(\tilde{x}_1 - (B/A)\tilde{x}_0\) — Kalman innovation: this is the component of \(x_1\) that is not explained by \(x_0\). If \(x_1\) is exactly what \(x_0\) predicts (via the AR(1) rule), the correction vanishes. If \(x_1\) is above/below the prediction, the score at frame 0 is pushed accordingly.
  • Interpretation: when generating frame 0, the model "looks ahead" to frame 1 to see if it is consistent. If frame 1 is unexpectedly large, the generator infers that \(a_0\) was probably also large, and steers \(x_0\) upward. This is the inter-frame information flow that was entirely absent in the old formulation.

04 The Kalman Smoother Connection

There is an equivalent way to write the joint score that reveals its deep structure. Recall from Mézard's board that the \(k\)-th component is: $$S_k(x, t) \;=\; \frac{e^{-t}\langle a_k \rangle_{a \mid x} - x_k}{\Delta_t}.$$ For our 2-frame Gaussian model, the posterior \(P(a_0, a_1 \mid x_0, x_1)\) is Gaussian. By the standard formula for conditional Gaussian distributions:

$$\begin{pmatrix}\langle a_0 \rangle \\ \langle a_1 \rangle\end{pmatrix}_{a \mid x} \;=\; \boldsymbol{\mu}_0 \;+\; e^{-t}\,\Sigma_0\,\Sigma_t^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{x}_0\\\tilde{x}_1\end{pmatrix}$$
Kalman smoother: posterior mean of clean frames given noisy observations. The matrix e^{-t}Σ₀Σ_t^{-1} is the Kalman gain (in smoother form). It is linear in x.

Let us verify this gives the same score. Inserting into \(S_k = (e^{-t}\langle a_k\rangle - x_k)/\Delta_t\):

$$e^{-t}\langle a \rangle - x \;=\; e^{-t}\boldsymbol{\mu}_0 \;+\; e^{-2t}\Sigma_0\Sigma_t^{-1}(x - e^{-t}\boldsymbol{\mu}_0) \;-\; x$$ $$= \;(e^{-2t}\Sigma_0\Sigma_t^{-1} - I)(x - e^{-t}\boldsymbol{\mu}_0)$$ $$= \;(e^{-2t}\Sigma_0 - \Sigma_t)\Sigma_t^{-1}(x - e^{-t}\boldsymbol{\mu}_0) \;=\; -\Delta_t\,\Sigma_t^{-1}(x - e^{-t}\boldsymbol{\mu}_0)$$
Key step: e^{-2t}Σ₀ − Σ_t = e^{-2t}Σ₀ − (e^{-2t}Σ₀ + Δ_t·I) = −Δ_t·I. Dividing by Δ_t recovers S = −Σ_t^{-1}(x − μ_t). The two expressions are identical. ✓
The Kalman smoother IS the propagator. The formula \(\langle a_k \rangle_{a|x} = \mu_{0,k} + e^{-t}(\Sigma_0\Sigma_t^{-1})_k \cdot (x - e^{-t}\boldsymbol{\mu}_0)\) is the exact smoother. Each row \(k\) of the matrix \(e^{-t}\Sigma_0\Sigma_t^{-1}\) tells you how to combine all observed frames to estimate the clean frame \(a_k\). This matrix — specifically, how its row structure changes with \(k\) — is the propagator.

05 Precision Matrix and Markov Structure

For K frames, the old AR(1) chain has a tridiagonal precision matrix \(\Sigma_0^{-1}\). Let us see this explicitly for K=2:

$$\Sigma_0^{-1} \;=\; \frac{1}{\sigma_0^2\sigma_\eta^2}\begin{pmatrix} \alpha^2\sigma_0^2+\sigma_\eta^2 & -\alpha\sigma_0^2 \\ -\alpha\sigma_0^2 & \sigma_0^2 \end{pmatrix}$$
Precision of the clean AR(1) chain. Off-diagonal entries are −α·(something), reflecting the Markov coupling. At t=0: Σ_t^{-1} = Σ_0^{-1} → this tridiagonal structure is the score's Jacobian.

After OU diffusion: \(\Sigma_t = e^{-2t}\Sigma_0 + \Delta_t I\). The precision \(\Sigma_t^{-1}\) is no longer tridiagonal for generic \(t\), but:

  • At \(t \to 0\): \(\Sigma_t \to \Sigma_0\), so \(\Sigma_t^{-1} \to \Sigma_0^{-1}\) → tridiagonal (Markov structure fully visible).
  • At \(t \to \infty\): \(\Sigma_t \to \Delta_t I\), so \(\Sigma_t^{-1} \to I/\Delta_t\) → diagonal (score decouples, \(\mathbf{S} \to -x/\Delta_t\)).
  • At intermediate \(t\): the off-diagonal entry of \(\Sigma_t^{-1}\) is \(-B/D = -\alpha\sigma_0^2 e^{-2t}/(AC-B^2)\), which decays monotonically with \(t\).

This decay of off-diagonal coupling with \(t\) is the quantitative version of the four-regime phase diagram from TM5 — but now in the correct joint picture.
