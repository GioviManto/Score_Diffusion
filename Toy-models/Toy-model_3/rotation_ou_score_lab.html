§1 The Two-Time Setup

We distinguish two notions of time:

  • Internal time \(u\in\{0,1,2,\dots\}\): the physical / semantic evolution (a Markov chain).
  • Diffusion time \(t\ge 0\): independent Ornstein–Uhlenbeck (OU) corruption of a fixed frame.

At each internal index \(u\), we have a clean state \(a(u)\in\mathbb{R}^2\). Then we corrupt it with OU diffusion to obtain an observation \(x\).

$$ x \;=\; e^{-t}\,a(u) \;+\; \sqrt{1-e^{-2t}}\,\varepsilon, \qquad \varepsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,I_2). $$
What we want to visualize: the per-frame score field \( s(x,u,t)=\nabla_x\log p_t(x\mid u) \), and how it changes as you move in \(u\) and \(t\).

§2 Clean Dynamics: Discrete Rotation + Noise

We start by sampling polar coordinates:

$$ r_0 \sim \mathcal N(\bar r,\sigma_r^2), \qquad \theta_0 \sim \mathrm{Unif}[0,2\pi), \qquad a(0)= \begin{pmatrix} r_0\cos\theta_0\\ r_0\sin\theta_0 \end{pmatrix}. $$

Then the internal-time evolution is a rotation by a fixed step \(\Delta\theta\) plus isotropic Gaussian noise:

$$ a(u+1)=R_{\Delta\theta}\,a(u)+\xi_u, \qquad \xi_u\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma_{\text{int}}^2\,I_2), $$ $$ R_{\Delta\theta}= \begin{pmatrix} \cos\Delta\theta & -\sin\Delta\theta\\ \sin\Delta\theta & \cos\Delta\theta \end{pmatrix}. $$
Rotation intuition: if \(\sigma_{\text{int}}=0\) then every particle follows a perfect circle and the angle increases by \(\Delta\theta\) each step. As \(\sigma_{\text{int}}\) grows, trajectories wander away from the circle.

§3 OU Corruption and the Score Field

Conditioned on a clean \(a(u)\), the OU transition kernel is Gaussian:

$$ p_t(x\mid a(u))=\mathcal N\!\Bigl(x;\; e^{-t}a(u),\;\sigma_t^2 I_2\Bigr), \qquad \sigma_t^2 = 1-e^{-2t}. $$

The marginal at internal index \(u\) is:

$$ p_t(x\mid u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^2} p_0(a\mid u)\; \mathcal N\!\Bigl(x;\;e^{-t}a,\;\sigma_t^2 I_2\Bigr)\,da. $$

The score is the gradient of the log density:

$$ s(x,u,t)=\nabla_x\log p_t(x\mid u). $$

In this HTML we approximate the integral with Monte Carlo samples \(\{a_i(u)\}_{i=1}^N\) from the internal-time Markov chain. Because every component shares the same covariance \(\sigma_t^2I_2\), we get an efficient “mixture score” formula:

$$ p_t(x\mid u)\approx \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N \phi(x;\mu_i,\sigma_t^2I_2), \qquad \mu_i = e^{-t}a_i(u), $$ $$ s(x,u,t)\approx \frac{1}{\sigma_t^2}\Bigl(\underbrace{\frac{\sum_i w_i(x)\,\mu_i}{\sum_j w_j(x)}}_{\text{soft assignment mean } \bar\mu(x)}-x\Bigr), \qquad w_i(x)=\exp\!\Bigl(-\frac{\lVert x-\mu_i\rVert^2}{2\sigma_t^2}\Bigr). $$
Limit check: as \(t\to\infty\), \(\mu_i=e^{-t}a_i(u)\to 0\) and \(\sigma_t^2\to 1\), so \(p_t(x\mid u)\) tends to a standard Gaussian and the score tends to \(s(x)\approx -x\).

§4 What You Should See

small diffusion time (t ≈ 0)

The Gaussian kernels are very sharp. The score field points toward the nearest “cluster” of mixture centers. The field can look spiky because \(p_t\) is close to the clean distribution.

large diffusion time (t large)

The OU corruption dominates. The density becomes nearly Gaussian and the score becomes nearly linear: \(s(x,u,t)\approx -x\).

Interpretation: you can think of the score as the “best local direction” that points back toward high-density regions of \(p_t(\cdot\mid u)\). The reverse-time diffusion SDE uses this field to denoise.
