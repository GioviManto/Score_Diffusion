Score-Based Generative Modeling · Analytical Laboratory

Score of a Joint Distribution
Under Internal Dynamics

A one-dimensional system with drift and noise: every quantity derived in closed form.

§1 Why This Model?

Many real-world systems evolve over an internal time — a natural temporal axis intrinsic to the data. Video frames, neural spike trains, and gene expression trajectories all carry this structure. When we observe such a system, each sample is not a single point but a sequence.

To build a generative model of such data, we need the score of a joint distribution that couples successive time points. In practice, a neural network learns this score. But to develop intuition — to see what the score looks like, how prior structure and temporal dynamics interact — we need a case where everything is known analytically.

The key idea: We define the simplest possible dynamic system — a constant drift plus Gaussian noise — over a trimodal prior. The joint distribution \(p(x_0, x_1)\) is a mixture of bivariate Gaussians, and its score decomposes cleanly into a prior term and a transition term.

§2 The Setup: Trimodal Prior

We choose a 1D Gaussian mixture with \(K = 3\) components as the starting data distribution:

$$p_0(x) = \sum_{k=1}^{3} w_k \, \mathcal{N}(x;\, \mu_k,\, \sigma_k^2)$$

Three modes with distinct means and spreads. This trimodal structure provides rich score geometry — the score of \(p_0\) oscillates between attractive basins centered at each mode. Concrete parameter values are configurable in the parameter panel below.

§3 The Dynamic Process

The system evolves via a simple additive process:

$$x_1 = x_0 + c + \eta, \qquad \eta \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_\eta^2)$$

Here \(c\) is a constant drift and \(\eta\) is independent Gaussian noise. The transition kernel is:

$$p(x_1 \mid x_0) = \mathcal{N}(x_1;\; x_0 + c,\; \sigma_\eta^2)$$

Applied repeatedly, this process shifts the distribution rightward by \(c\) per step while gradually diffusing it. After \(t\) steps, the marginal is:

$$p_t(x) = \sum_{k=1}^{3} w_k \, \mathcal{N}\!\left(x;\; \mu_k + tc,\; \sigma_k^2 + t\,\sigma_\eta^2\right)$$
Two competing effects. The drift \(tc\) shifts all modes uniformly, preserving relative spacing. The accumulated noise \(t\,\sigma_\eta^2\) broadens each component, eventually causing the modes to merge into a single wide hump.

§4 The Joint Distribution

The joint density of two consecutive time points factorizes as prior times transition:

$$p(x_0, x_1) = p_0(x_0) \cdot p(x_1 \mid x_0)$$

Substituting the mixture prior and the Gaussian kernel, each term in the sum is a product of two 1D Gaussians — which is itself a 2D Gaussian. The result is a mixture of bivariate Gaussians:

$$p(x_0, x_1) = \sum_{k=1}^{3} w_k \, \mathcal{N}_2\!\left(\begin{pmatrix}x_0\\x_1\end{pmatrix};\; \boldsymbol{\mu}_k,\; \Sigma_k\right)$$

with component parameters:

Joint mean

$$\boldsymbol{\mu}_k = \begin{pmatrix}\mu_k \\ \mu_k + c\end{pmatrix}$$

All three means lie on the line \(x_1 = x_0 + c\). The drift sets the diagonal offset.

Joint covariance

$$\Sigma_k = \begin{pmatrix}\sigma_k^2 & \sigma_k^2 \\ \sigma_k^2 & \sigma_k^2 + \sigma_\eta^2\end{pmatrix}$$

The off-diagonal entry \(\sigma_k^2\) encodes perfect correlation through the deterministic part of the process.

The determinant of each covariance is \(\det(\Sigma_k) = \sigma_k^2 \, \sigma_\eta^2\), giving a well-defined inverse:

$$\Sigma_k^{-1} = \frac{1}{\sigma_k^2 \, \sigma_\eta^2}\begin{pmatrix}\sigma_k^2 + \sigma_\eta^2 & -\sigma_k^2 \\ -\sigma_k^2 & \sigma_k^2\end{pmatrix}$$

§5 The Score of the Joint

The score — the gradient of the log-density — is a 2D vector field over the \((x_0, x_1)\) plane:

$$\nabla \log p(x_0, x_1) = \begin{pmatrix} \partial_{x_0} \log p \\ \partial_{x_1} \log p \end{pmatrix} = \Big [ \frac{\partial}{\partial x_0} \log p(x_0)+\frac{x_1 - x_0 - c}{\sigma^2} \quad \quad \frac{x_0 + c- x_1}{\sigma^2} \Big ]^T$$

§6 Decomposition: Prior Score + Transition Score

Because the joint factorizes as \(p(x_0,x_1) = p_0(x_0) \cdot p(x_1|x_0)\), the log-joint splits additively:

$$\log p(x_0,x_1) = \underbrace{\log p_0(x_0)}_{\text{prior}} \;-\; \underbrace{\frac{(x_1 - x_0 - c)^2}{2\sigma_\eta^2}}_{\text{transition}} \;+\; \text{const}$$

Differentiating with respect to each variable yields a clean decomposition of the score:

\(x_0\)-component

$$s_{x_0} = \underbrace{\nabla_{x_0}\!\log p_0(x_0)}_{\text{prior score}} + \underbrace{\frac{x_1 - x_0 - c}{\sigma_\eta^2}}_{\text{transition pull}}$$

Two competing forces: the prior pulls \(x_0\) toward mode centers; the transition pulls it toward \(x_1 - c\).

\(x_1\)-component

$$s_{x_1} = -\frac{x_1 - x_0 - c}{\sigma_\eta^2}$$

Purely from the transition kernel. Always points toward \(x_0 + c\) — the expected position given \(x_0\).

The prior score of the mixture is itself a weighted average:

$$\nabla_{x_0}\!\log p_0(x_0) = \frac{\displaystyle\sum_k w_k\,\varphi_k(x_0)\,\frac{\mu_k - x_0}{\sigma_k^2}}{\displaystyle\sum_k w_k\,\varphi_k(x_0)}$$

where \(\varphi_k(x_0) = \mathcal{N}(x_0;\,\mu_k,\,\sigma_k^2)\).

Bayesian reading. The \(x_0\) score balances two sources of information: the prior (what values of \(x_0\) are likely a priori) and the likelihood (consistency between \(x_0\) and the observed \(x_1\)). The \(x_1\) score is purely a "correction" — it says "given \(x_0\), move \(x_1\) toward \(x_0 + c\)."

§10 Summary

What this toy model shows. For a system with internal dynamics, the score of the joint distribution decomposes into a prior term (encoding the data distribution) and a transition term (encoding the temporal law). The \(x_1\)-component of the score is entirely determined by the transition kernel — it always corrects \(x_1\) toward \(x_0 + c\). The \(x_0\)-component balances the prior's pull toward mode centers against consistency with the observed future. This clean separation is what a score-matching network must learn to reproduce.
